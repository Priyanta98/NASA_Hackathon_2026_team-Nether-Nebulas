MISSION GUIDE FOR EVERYONE

How We Track Distant Solar-System Worlds

Clear, step-by-step physics explaining how SPHEREx spots moving objects in the outer Solar System.

STEP 01

Why Distant Worlds Shift Against Stars

Distant stars stay locked at fixed coordinates on the sky. Solar-system objects—like asteroids, Centaurs, and icy Kuiper Belt bodies—orbit the Sun much closer to us, so comparing images taken across different dates reveals a moving dot against stationary stars.

STEP 02

How Drift Speed Reveals Distance in AU

As Earth orbits the Sun at 30 km/s, our viewing angle shifts. Nearby asteroids cross dozens of arcseconds per day, whereas worlds 50 to 100+ AU away in the Scattered Disk drift only a few arcseconds per day. Measuring that rate gives a reliable distance range in Astronomical Units (AU).

STEP 03

How Motion-Tracked Spectra Detect Ice

SPHEREx measures 102 infrared wavelengths (0.75–5.0 μm) across consecutive exposures. By shifting our measurement window along the moving object's path, we keep the object centered and detect the 3.1 μm absorption signature of frozen water and methane ice.
